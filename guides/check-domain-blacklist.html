How to Check If a Domain Is Blacklisted

Domains can be blacklisted too, on URI lists and domain reputation lists. Learn how to check a domain, read the results, and clear a domain listing.

IP lists and domain lists are different

Most people check IP blacklists and stop there, but domains have their own reputation systems. A URI blacklist lists domains that appear in spam messages, while domain reputation lists track domains associated with phishing, malware, or spam campaigns. A domain can be listed even when the IP that hosts it is completely clean, and a clean domain can sit on a listed IP. If your mail is filtered despite a clean IP check, the domain is the next place to look. Check the sending domain, the domains in your message links, and any redirect or tracking domains you use, because any one of them can trigger a filter.

How to check a domain

Start with a domain blacklist checker that queries URI lists such as SURBL and URIBL, and also check the domain against safe-browsing and phishing lists run by Google and other security vendors. Enter the domain without any path or protocol, just example.com, and review each result separately. Note which lists flag the domain and what category they assign, such as spam, phishing, or malware, because the category determines the fix. A phishing flag is far more serious than a spam flag and needs immediate investigation. Keep a dated record of the results, as you would for an IP check, and check related subdomains too if your mail uses them.

Why domains get listed

Domains are listed for the same broad reasons as IPs: they were used to send spam, they appeared in spam messages, they hosted malicious content, or they were used in a phishing campaign. A hacked website is a common cause: attackers add hidden spam pages to a legitimate site, and those pages get reported and listed. Expired domains that are re-registered by spammers can also carry a bad history that the new owner inherits. Another cause is a compromised email account that sends spam containing links to your domain, which then gets associated with the campaign. In each case the fix starts with removing the abusive content or closing the compromised account.

Clearing a domain listing

The process mirrors IP delisting, but the evidence is different. Clean the hacked pages, remove the malicious content, secure the accounts, and check the site with a malware scanner. Then submit a removal request to each list operator, explaining what was compromised, what was cleaned, and when. For Google Safe Browsing, use the Search Console security review process, which requires you to verify ownership of the site. For URI lists, use the operator's own lookup and removal page. Do not request removal before the site is clean, because a re-check that still finds malicious content will be refused and may make the next request harder.

Protecting your domain reputation

Publish SPF, DKIM, and DMARC so your domain cannot easily be forged in spam, and monitor your DMARC reports for unauthorised use. Keep your website software updated, use strong passwords and two-factor authentication, and scan the site regularly for malware. Register common lookalike domains if your brand is a phishing target, and set up alerts for new mentions of your domain in security feeds. Check the domain's listing status monthly, or weekly if you send a lot of mail. A domain with a long clean history is a valuable asset, and protecting it is far cheaper than rebuilding it after a listing.

Frequently asked questions

Can a domain be listed if the IP is clean?

Yes. Domain lists and IP lists are separate systems, and a domain can be flagged for spam or phishing while its hosting IP stays clean.

How do I check a domain in a link?

Enter the bare domain from the link, without the path. Check redirect and tracking domains separately, since they have their own reputations.

How long does domain delisting take?

Usually a few hours to a few days after the site is clean and the request is submitted, though Google Safe Browsing reviews can take longer.
