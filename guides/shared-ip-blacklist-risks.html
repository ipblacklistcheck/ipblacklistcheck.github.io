Shared IP Blacklist Risks: When a Neighbour Gets You Listed

Shared hosting and shared mail IPs are cheap, but one bad neighbour can list everyone. Learn the risks, the warning signs, and your options.

How shared IPs work

On shared hosting, many websites and mailboxes share a single outbound IP address to keep costs down. The hosting provider routes all of their mail through that address, and receivers see only the shared IP, not which customer sent each message. This is efficient and affordable, and for small sites with low mail volume it usually works fine. The risk is structural: reputation is attached to the IP, not to the individual customer, so every customer on that IP shares one reputation. If one customer behaves badly, the reputation of the whole group falls, and every customer pays the price.

How a neighbour can list you

It takes only one compromised account or one deliberate spammer on a shared IP to trigger a listing. The spammer sends bulk mail, recipients complain, list operators notice the volume, and the shared address is added to a blacklist. Legitimate customers discover the problem when their own mail starts bouncing or landing in spam, even though they sent nothing unusual. The listing notice names the IP, not the culprit, so innocent users often spend hours checking their own accounts before realising the cause sits with another customer on the same server. Hosting providers can usually identify the offender from their logs, but they may be slow to act.

Warning signs to watch for

The first sign is usually a sudden rise in bounces or spam-folder placement without any change in your own sending. A weekly blacklist check that suddenly turns positive is another clear signal. Check whether the listing appeared at a time when your own volume was normal, and look at your mail logs for anything unusual. If your logs are clean, the cause is likely elsewhere on the shared IP. Ask your hosting provider whether other customers share the address and whether they have had abuse reports. A provider that cannot or will not answer that question is a warning sign in itself.

Your options when it happens

Start by reporting the listing to your hosting provider with the date, the list name, and your clean logs. Ask them to identify and suspend the abusive account and to request delisting. A good provider will act within hours. If the problem repeats, ask to be moved to a different shared IP pool, ideally one reserved for mail with a better reputation. For businesses that depend on email, the stronger fix is a dedicated IP address or a reputable third-party mail service, where your reputation is yours alone. A dedicated IP costs more and needs careful warm-up, but it removes the neighbour risk entirely.

Choosing a host with mail in mind

When you choose hosting, ask specific questions about mail: does mail share an IP with web traffic, how many customers share an outbound IP, how quickly does the abuse team act, and can you get a dedicated IP if you need one. Read reviews that mention email delivery, not just uptime and price. A host that monitors its mail IPs, acts quickly on abuse, and offers dedicated IPs is worth paying more for if email matters to your business. Cheap hosting that cannot deliver your mail is not cheap at all. Keep a weekly blacklist check in your routine so a neighbour problem is caught in days, not months.

Frequently asked questions

Can I find out which neighbour caused the listing?

Usually not directly. Only your hosting provider can see per-customer logs, so ask them to investigate and confirm the cause.

Will a dedicated IP guarantee I stay clean?

No, but it removes the neighbour risk. You still need good authentication, a clean list, and secure accounts to stay off blacklists.

Should I leave a host after one shared listing?

Not necessarily after one incident if the host fixes it fast. Repeated listings with slow responses are a good reason to move.
