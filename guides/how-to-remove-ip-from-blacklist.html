How to Get Your IP Removed from a Blacklist

Removed from a blacklist step by step: fix the cause, gather evidence, submit a delisting request, and monitor afterwards. What works, what delays removal, and what to avoid.

Fix the cause before you ask for removal

List operators remove addresses when they are convinced the problem is fixed, not simply because someone asked. Before you submit any request, find and stop the source of the bad traffic. Check for compromised mailboxes, change weak passwords, remove malicious scripts, close open relays, and patch outdated software. Look at your mail logs for the period before the listing appeared and identify what changed: a new plugin, a new user, a sudden volume spike, or a spike in failed logins. If you cannot find the cause, ask your hosting provider or mail administrator for help, because requesting removal while spam is still flowing will almost certainly be refused, and a second listing soon after removal is harder to clear.

Gather the evidence you will need

A good delisting request is short, honest, and specific. Note the IP address, the date you discovered the listing, what caused it, what you changed, and when the bad traffic stopped. Include log excerpts if the operator asks for them, but do not send passwords or unrelated personal data. If the cause was a compromised account, say which account, when it was secured, and what password and security changes were made. If the cause was a misconfiguration, describe the setting that was wrong and how it is now set. Operators deal with thousands of requests, so a clear timeline and a concrete list of fixes is far more persuasive than a long apology or a claim that you did nothing wrong.

Submit the request to each operator separately

There is no central delisting form. Each blacklist has its own process, and you must follow it for every list that includes your address. Spamhaus, SpamCop, Barracuda, and SORBS each publish their own removal pages and requirements. Read the instructions on the operator's own site, complete the form carefully, and use a real contact address that you monitor, because some operators reply with questions or a verification step. Do not use a removal service that promises instant delisting for a fee unless you have checked its reputation carefully. Many such services simply submit the same public form you could submit yourself, and some are outright scams.

Wait, monitor, and do not spam the operator

Most delisting decisions take a few hours to a few days. Some lists remove an address automatically once they stop seeing bad traffic, while others require manual review. During the wait, keep your mail volume low and clean, and do not send test messages in bulk to see if delivery works. Repeated requests to the same operator can slow the process or be treated as abuse. Check the listing status once a day, and keep a record of each request date and any reference number you receive. If a request is refused, read the reason carefully. It usually points to a problem that is still active, such as ongoing spam, a missing reverse DNS record, or a policy issue with your mail setup.

Prevent the next listing

Removal is only half the job. After delisting, set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC if you have not already, so receivers can verify your mail is genuine. Enable two-factor authentication on mail accounts, keep software updated, and limit who can send bulk mail from your server. Set up a weekly blacklist check and a bounce-rate alert, and review your mail logs regularly for unusual volume. If you share an IP with other customers, consider moving to a dedicated IP so another tenant's behaviour cannot list you again. Good hygiene is cheaper than repeated removals, and a clean history makes any future delisting request much easier.

Frequently asked questions

How long does removal usually take?

Most lists decide within a few hours to three days. Automatic lists can clear within a day of the spam stopping; manual review lists can take longer.

Can I pay to get removed faster?

Legitimate operators do not sell removal. Be wary of any service that promises instant delisting for payment; it is usually submitting the free public form on your behalf.

What if my request is refused?

Read the refusal reason, fix the remaining problem, wait for the operator's stated period, and then submit a fresh request with evidence of the fix.
